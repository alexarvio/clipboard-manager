COMMON QUESTIONS

Windows clipboard manager, answered

Does Windows 11 have a built-in clipboard manager?

Yes — press Win+V to open Windows' built-in clipboard history. It keeps up to 25 items, has no search, and clears everything on restart unless you've individually pinned each item. For light, occasional use it's fine; for anyone copying text throughout the day it runs out of room fast.

How do I get more than 25 items in Windows clipboard history?

Windows' native Win+V clipboard caps out at 25 items with no setting to raise it. To keep a longer, searchable history you need a third-party clipboard manager like FatClipboard, which stores unlimited items on Pro (100 items free) and keeps them across restarts.

Is there a free clipboard manager for Windows?

Yes. FatClipboard's free tier includes 100 items of history (or 7 days, whichever comes first), instant search, folders, and screenshot OCR search at no cost — no account charge, no trial required. Pro adds unlimited history and AI features starting at $3.99/mo.

Can I search my clipboard history on Windows?

Not with Windows' built-in Win+V panel — it's the one pane in that flyout without a search box. FatClipboard adds instant text search as you type, plus optional AI-powered semantic search that finds items by meaning, not just exact wording.

Does a clipboard manager slow down my PC?

A well-built one shouldn't. FatClipboard sits idle listening for clipboard changes and does nothing until you copy something or press the hotkey — no background indexing job, no constant disk scanning, no upload.

Is my clipboard data private?

Your clipboard history is stored only in a local database on your own PC and is never uploaded. The one exception is the optional AI features (transform and smart search): the specific snippet you act on is sent to be processed and the result returned, and it isn't logged.
